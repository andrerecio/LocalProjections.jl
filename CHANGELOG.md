# Changelog

## Unreleased (andrerecio fork, port onto 0.2)

### Added

- **Long differences**: `ldiff(y)` (``y_{t+h} - y_{t-1}``, Piger & Stockwell 2025), `hbr(x, y)` (``(x_{t+h} - x_{t-1})/y_{t-1}``, Hall 2009, Barro & Redlick 2011; `hbr(x)` scales by `x` itself), and `cumul` of either, which sums the changes over horizons `0..h` with the baseline counted `h + 1` times. `firstdiff(x)` gives the lagged-difference controls `lags(firstdiff(y), p)`. A long difference on the left is never treated as a tautological `h = 0`. `cumul(ldiff(y, 3))` is an error; write `cumul(ldiff(y), 3)`.
- **Horizon-tracking regressors**: a bare `cumul(x)`, `leads(x)`, `ldiff(x)` or `hbr(x, y)` on the right-hand side (exogenous, endogenous or instrument) is rebuilt at every horizon, under one coefficient name, so the one-step cumulative multiplier is a single call, `lpiv(TSLS(), @formula(cumul(y) ~ (cumul(g) ~ z) + ...), df; horizon)`. In panels, these terms are built within each unit. An explicit horizon (`cumul(x, 3)`) pins the column.

- **EWC inference with Student-t_B critical values**: bands from `summarize`, `as_irf_result` and the plots use the `t_B` quantile when the stored estimator is `EWC(B)` (fixed-smoothing inference, Lazarus, Lewis, Stock & Watson 2018), and the normal quantile for every other estimator. `ewc_bandwidth(lp)` gives the LLSW rule `B = ⌊0.41·T₀^(2/3)⌋` with `T₀` the horizon-0 sample. EWC standard errors carry Regress's `n/(n−k)` factor (on `lp` and `lpiv` alike), so they are `√(n/(n−k))` times Stata `harreg`'s; the critical values are identical.

- **State-dependent projections** (Ramey & Zubairy 2018): `lp`/`lpiv` take `state`, `statelag` and `regimes`. Every block of the design (exogenous incl. the intercept, endogenous, instruments) is split as `[B .* (1 - I_{t-lag}), B .* I_{t-lag}]`, with regime-suffixed names (`x_s0`/`x_s1`, or `x_exp`/`x_rec`); the state may be a weight in `[0, 1]`, is lagged within each panel unit, and its `missing` values exclude rows. The result carries an `LPState` in its `state` field. `statetest(lp[, estimator])` tests equal regime coefficients horizon by horizon from the full stored covariance (`χ²₁`, or `F(1, B)` under EWC), and `weakivtest(lpiv, h; regime)` runs Montiel Olea–Pflueger on the one-regime regression. Combining `state` with `fe()` is an error. Verified against `ivreg2`/`weakivtest` (Stata 19.5) on the Ramey–Zubairy data, with Regress 0.3's `n/(n−k)` on kernel HAC taken into account.

- **VAR lag-order selection**: `lagselect(data, vars; maxlags, criterion = :aic | :bic)` ports `ic_var.m` of the Montiel Olea, Plagborg-Møller, Qian & Wolf (2025) suite (penalty denominator fixed at `T − p_max`, `Σ̂_p` from the full sample); `nlags(sel)` gives the selected order, and `VARLagSelection` is a Tables.jl table. Any Tables.jl input; `missing`/`NaN` in the VAR columns are rejected. The VAR primitives behind it and the coming bootstraps (`_var_ols`, Pope's bias correction, impact vector, IRF and MA recursions, simulation, Cholesky FEVD) are in `src/var.jl`. `LinearAlgebra` becomes a dependency.

- **Herbst–Johannsen bias correction**: `biascorrect(lp)` returns a `BiasCorrectedLP`, an `AbstractLocalProjection` whose `coefpath` is the corrected path `θ̂ᶜ_h = θ̂_h + (T₀ − h)⁻¹ Σⱼ ĉⱼ θ̂ᶜ_{h−j}` (port of `lp_biascorr.m`). `summarize`, `as_irf_result` and the plots center on the corrected path with the standard errors of the uncorrected coefficients, from the stored estimator; `bc + vcov(est)` keeps the correction, and `as_irf_result` flags `bias_corrected = true`. The correction throws where it is not derived: IV, `sample = :fixed`, panels, `fe()`, state dependence, horizon-tracking regressors, `ldiff`/`hbr` responses, and samples that are not the horizon-0 sample truncated by `h` rows.

- **VAR residual moving-block bootstrap**: `varbootstrap(lp, data; vars, nlags, nboot, blocklength, biascorrect = true, popecorrect = true, rng, threaded)` ports the Montiel Olea, Plagborg-Møller, Qian & Wolf (2025) procedure (`var_boot.m`, `var_sim.m`, `boot_ci.m`): Pope-corrected VAR DGP, sliding-window recentered blocks, the full local projection re-estimated in every draw (Herbst–Johannsen-corrected by default), and pointwise `:hall_t` (Hall percentile-t), `:hall` or `:efron` bands from `summarize(b; level, method)`. The result's plots (`irfplot(b)`, `plot(b)`) and `as_irf_result(b)` are drawn from one `LocalProjectionIRFResult` carrying the bootstrap bands. Per-draw seeds make threaded and serial runs bit-identical. Single-series OLS projections with `leads`/`cumul` responses only; panels, `time`, `fe()`, state dependence, horizon-tracking regressors and IV throw, and `sample = :fixed` needs `biascorrect = false`.

- **Forecast-error variance decompositions** (Gorodnichenko & Lee 2020): `fevd(formula, data; horizon, estimator = :r2 | :lpa | :lpb, irf_formula, dofadjust, trend)` and `fevd(lp_result, data)` port `SubR2.m`, `SubLPA.m`, `SubLPB.m` with the per-horizon asymptotic variances of `SimulationAVar*.m`, on a private QR matrix core that keeps the reference conventions. `fevdbootstrap(f[, data]; method = :var | :block | :simulation)` gives the bias corrections and bands (`summarize(b; band = :shift | :normal)`); `FEVDSummary` is a Tables.jl table. Plots.jl recipes keep the FEVD layout (y-axis over [0, 1], the uncorrected estimate dashed next to a bias-corrected centre); `irfplot(f)`/`irfplot(b)` (Makie) and `as_irf_result` go through a `LocalProjectionIRFResult` of the shares. Any Tables.jl table with rows in time order; `fevd(lp_result, data)` throws for `sample = :fixed`, panels and `time`, as for IV and state dependence. Parity with the authors' MATLAB code is tested against Octave fixtures generated locally (`test/fixtures/goro_lee/`, the `:octave` items are skipped without them). Tutorial: `docs/src/tutorials/fevd.md`.

- **Ramey–Zubairy tutorial** (`docs/src/tutorials/ramey_zubairy.md`): replication of the Ramey–Zubairy (2018) cumulative multipliers with `lpiv`, the two-step multiplier, EWC bands, bias-corrected bootstrap bands and state dependence, with five figures regenerated by `docs/make_rz_figure.jl`, `make_rz_ewc_figure.jl`, `make_rz_state_figure.jl` and `make_rz_bootstrap_figure.jl`. Ported from the fork's `dev`: the point estimates are unchanged, while the `lpiv` HAC standard errors are 1–3% larger than there, because Regress 0.3 applies `n/(n−K)` to the long-run variance on IV models too (so the `statetest` p-values move slightly).
- **Bootstrap coverage study and benchmarks**: `benchmark/coverage_study.jl` (run by hand: `julia +1.13 --project=benchmark -t 4 benchmark/coverage_study.jl [nmc nboot]`) checks the coverage of the `varbootstrap` bands against a known VAR(1) impulse response and what the Pope and Herbst–Johannsen corrections buy; its default run reproduces on 0.2 every figure quoted in its docstring. `benchmark/benchmarks.jl` adds `lagselect` and `varbootstrap` benchmarks.
- **Inference procedures guide** (`docs/src/inference_procedures_guide.md`, in the manual): the mathematics of EWC, the Herbst–Johannsen correction and the VAR moving-block bootstrap, mapped to the Montiel Olea–Plagborg-Møller–Qian–Wolf replication code, with the audit of 0.1.0 kept as a record. A status note and dated updates record that its findings are resolved on 0.2 (LP-1 upstream, REG-1 in Regress.jl 0.3, EWC-1 here), and the §8 bandwidth tables are recomputed with the IV `n/(n−k)` factor. Its math now renders in Documenter (` ```math ` blocks and double-backtick inline math in place of `\[…\]` and `$…$`).
- **README** for the port, from the fork's `dev` README: installation on Julia 1.13, a section on upstream 0.2's panels, fixed effects and `sample = :fixed`, the stored-estimator API (`lp_result + vcov(estimator)`), `lpiv(TSLS(), …)`, and the Ramey–Zubairy and Piger–Stockwell worked examples with every quoted number rechecked on 0.2 (the IV factor `n/(n−k)` moves the state-test p-values and the HBR band slightly). The Piger–Stockwell example and `docs/make_ps_figure.jl` keep the shock `missing` before 1990:2 instead of recoding it to `NaN`, since 0.2 builds lags on the original time axis (identical results). The limitations section drops the `missing`-value workaround and records the Regress.jl requirement.
- **Quarto site** (`docs/quarto`, published at andrearecine.me/LocalProjections.jl): the overview, Ramey–Zubairy and Piger–Stockwell pages from the old fork `main`, ported to the 0.2 API (`lpiv(TSLS(), …)`, the per-horizon `weakivtest(lpiv)` with `nothing` where there is no first stage, the IV `n/(n−k)` factor in the text, `missing` shocks kept in Piger–Stockwell). Every number on the pages is computed at render time.

- **Joint inference across horizons** (lecture 3 of Plagborg-Møller's 2026 local-projection course): `jointvcov(lp[, estimator]; term, horizons)` is the covariance of the impulse-response path across horizons, from the scores of every horizon stacked on one time axis (Stata's `suest`), under the stored estimator (HC0–HC5, kernel HAC or EWC), with the pointwise standard errors on its diagonal. `jointtest` is the Wald test of the response at several horizons (`χ²`, or the fixed-smoothing `F(m, B−m+1)` for `EWC(B)`), returning a `JointTest`. `supt_critical_value` and `bands = :supt` in `summarize`, `plot`, `irfplot` and `as_irf_result` give the sup-t simultaneous band of Montiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller (2019), simulated with the quantile rule of Kessler's `estimate_supt_critical_value` (for EWC from the Wishart fixed-b limit). OLS and IV, single series; panels, `fe()` and cluster/Driscoll–Kraay estimators throw. Verified through PyStata against the course's `suest`/`test` (exact after `N/(N−1)` vs the per-horizon factors) and against `estimate_supt_critical_value` on the same draws.
- **Implicit-shock variance decomposition** (lecture 1 of the course): `implicitshock(lp)` is the Frisch–Waugh residual of the impulse on all the controls, and `implicitfevd(lp)` returns an `ImplicitFEVD` with the `R²` of each horizon's forecast error (response on the controls) on the current and future implicit shocks. It matches the course's Stata code to 1e-13. `docs/src/data/bilal_kanzig.csv` is the course's Bilal–Känzig extract; the Quarto page `lp-course.qmd` works through both lectures on it.

### Changed

- **EWC bands are wider than before**: upstream 0.2 used normal critical values for EWC too. This is a deliberate methodological change.

### Fixed

- `lags(x, p)` of an integer column (a 0/1 dummy read from CSV) no longer throws `InexactError: Int64(NaN)`: integer columns are converted to `Float64` before the design is built, as columns with `missing` already were.
- `anchor(y, lag(y))` (any anchor that is `missing` in its first rows) no longer throws when converting the anchored response; it equals `ldiff(y)`.
- `weakivtest(lpiv, h; regime)` works with Regress.jl's unified estimation path (Regress.jl pull request 24, merged 2026-10-24), whose matrix IV fits store the endogenous columns as `endo_cols` and may drop collinear columns or recategorize an endogenous regressor as exogenous; it threw a `FieldError` before. When that leaves no even split of the endogenous regressors and instruments between the two regimes (an instrument equal to its regressor, as Blanchard–Perotti at h = 0), it throws an `ArgumentError`. With that Regress, `weakivtest(lpiv, h)` on such a horizon throws for linear projections too (with a message that says why), where it returned a numerically infinite F; `weakivtest(lpiv)` over all horizons returns `nothing` there, as for a tautological horizon 0, with or without `regime`.
- The documentation builds again: `docs/src/api.md` lists every exported docstring (it was missing 18, including `lpiv`, `horizonmodel`, `as_irf_result` and the `irfplot` methods) and no longer asks for a `lags` docstring, which Regress.jl does not provide; the `@ref`s that pointed to undocumented names resolve. The manual adds the Plotting and FEVD tutorials and an EWC section in the inference tutorial; `pagesonly = true` skips stray `.md` files.

## 0.2.0

This release breaks the 0.1 API. No deprecation shims are provided.

### Added

- **Panel local projections**: `lp` and `lpiv` accept `unit` and `time` column names. Rows are sorted by unit and time, and leads, cumulative sums, anchors and lags are built within each unit. Repeated (unit, time) pairs, a unit that skips a period present elsewhere in the data, missing or NaN identifiers, and `unit` without `time` are errors. Panel results default to `CR1(unit)` standard errors.
- **Clustered and Driscoll-Kraay inference by column name**: `lp + vcov(CR1(:id))` and `lp + vcov(DriscollKraay(K; tis = :t, iis = :id))` build the estimator for each horizon from the rows that horizon uses.
- **Absorbed fixed effects**: `fe(column)` terms in `lp` and `lpiv` formulas, e.g. `leads(y) ~ x + fe(id) + fe(t)`, are absorbed by Regress at each horizon on that horizon's rows. The model has no intercept, and the absorbed levels enter the degrees of freedom and the clustered small-sample factor (a fixed effect nested in the cluster counts once). `fe` is exported. Fixed effects interacted with other terms or placed inside `(endogenous ~ instruments)` are errors.
- **Tables.jl input**: `lp` and `lpiv` take any Tables.jl table. String and categorical columns enter through StatsModels contrasts. `missing` in a numeric column excludes the row only at the horizons that need it.
- **`sample = :fixed`**: estimates every horizon on the rows available at all horizons. The default, `:varying`, keeps per-horizon samples.
- **`horizonmodel(lp, h)`**: returns the Regress model at horizon `h` in `0:lp.horizon`. Out-of-range horizons throw an `ArgumentError`. The model carries the formula's coefficient names, so `coefnames(horizonmodel(lp, h)) == coefnames(lp)` and `coeftable` prints them; first-stage models from `first_stage(lpiv, h)` are named by the exogenous regressors and instruments.
- **`nobs(lp)`** and **`stderror(lp; term)`**: per-horizon observation counts and standard errors, as methods on the StatsAPI generics.
- **`AbstractLocalProjection`**: exported supertype of `LocalProjection` and `LocalProjectionIV`.
- **`IRFSummary` is a Tables.jl table**, with `response`, `term`, `level` and `scale` columns, so `DataFrame(summary)` works without DataFrames as a dependency.

### Changed

- **`lpiv` takes the IV estimator first**: `lpiv(TSLS(), formula, data; horizon)`, mirroring `Regress.iv`. `TSLS()`, `LIML()`, `Fuller(a)` and `KClass(kappa)` are accepted and re-exported; each horizon is fitted by Regress's matrix `iv` for that estimator and matches the Regress formula fit.
- **Inference uses the stored estimator**: `summarize`, `as_irf_result` and the plotting functions use the estimator set by `lp + vcov(estimator)` (HC1 by default). Passing an estimator is the same as applying it with `+ vcov(estimator)` first.
- **One computation of IRF bands**: `summarize`, `as_irf_result` and the plots all build their numbers from one `LocalProjectionIRFResult`.
- **Plots are drawn by MacroEconometricTools**: `irfplot`, `irfplot!` and the Plots recipe hand the IRF object to MacroEconometricTools' Makie methods and Plots recipe. `irfplot!` draws onto any `Axis`.
- **Default shock is chosen by name**: the first endogenous variable, otherwise the first coefficient other than the intercept. Formulas without an intercept (`0 + x`) work.
- **`weakivtest(lpiv, 0)` throws** when the response is the endogenous shock; `weakivtest(lpiv)` returns `nothing` at horizon 0.
- **Per-horizon R² is centered when the formula has an intercept**: each horizon model, and each first-stage regression behind `first_stage`, is fitted with Regress's `has_intercept` set from the formula, so `r2` and `adjr2` equal those of the Regress formula fit. Formulas without an intercept keep the uncentered R². Coefficients, standard errors and first-stage F statistics are unchanged.
- **Pipe syntax `y|z`** becomes an `AnchorTerm` only inside `lp` and `lpiv`; other packages' formulas keep the StatsModels meaning of `|`.
- **`first_stage` and `weakivtest` extend the Regress.jl functions**, and `irfplot!` is the MacroEconometricTools function.
- **`lags()` / `LagTerm` come from Regress.jl.**
- **Requires CovarianceMatrices 0.33.1, Regress 0.3, MacroEconometricTools 0.2, and Julia 1.13.** Julia 1.13 is the floor that Regress and MacroEconometricTools set.

### Removed

- `LocalProjectionCovariance`, `vcov(estimator, lp)` and `stderror(cov; term)`. `vcov` and `stderror` are the StatsAPI functions.
- `coefnames(lp, h)`; `coefnames(lp)` covers all horizons.
- The Makie extension, the `IRFPlot` recipe and `irfplot_axis`.
- `irf_scale` and `flipshock` plotting keywords. Use `rescale` from MacroEconometricTools.jl before plotting.
- Exports of `FirstStageIV`, `WeakIVTestResult` (public in Regress) and `LocalProjectionIRFResult` (public in MacroEconometricTools). `CumulTerm`, `LeadTerm` and `AnchorTerm` are `public` but no longer exported.
